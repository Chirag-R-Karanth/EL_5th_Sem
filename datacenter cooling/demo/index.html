05 · Cooling strategy comparison

Conventional room air

Baseline

CRAC/CRAH units condition the room. Mixing and bypass airflow can reduce effectiveness.

Strength: mature, broadly deployable.

Constraint: difficult airflow management at high rack density.

Hot/cold aisle containment

Airflow optimization

Physical separation limits mixing of hot exhaust and cold supply air.

Strength: retrofit-friendly; improves usable cooling capacity.

Constraint: containment quality and blanking panels matter.

Direct-to-chip liquid

High-density option

Coolant removes heat near CPUs/GPUs, reducing dependence on room air for the dominant heat source.

Strength: supports high heat flux and dense compute.

Constraint: plumbing, leak management and facility integration.

07 · Model and interpretation

Q̇IT ≈ PIT
Q̇cooling = PIT / COP
PUE ≈ 1 + Pcooling / PIT

Simplified thermal response:

T(t) = Tin + ΔTss(1 − e^(−t/τ))

Steady-state temperature rise is scaled by IT load, cooling capacity and airflow effectiveness. τ represents thermal response time.

Containment improves airflow delivery and reduces hot/cold mixing. Liquid cooling changes the heat-capture point. Neither guarantees lower total facility energy: pump power, heat rejection and operating conditions must be measured.
Model disclosure: All displayed values and charts are generated by a simplified illustrative model. COP values, inlet temperature, thermal time constant and rack variation are assumptions. They are not measured results, validated CFD, or a substitute for experimental data. Replace assumptions with sensor data for the final study.
